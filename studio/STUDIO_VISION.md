# Studio Vision

## Mission
Turn real scientific discoveries into short games people *want* to play, then introduce them to the scientists behind the ideas.

## Guiding principle
**Science is not the subject of the game. Science is the mechanic.**
A player should first enjoy the game, and only afterwards realise: *"Wait, a real animal or system actually does that?"*

## What our games are
- **Short:** 7–10 minutes to complete. Respect the player's time.
- **Playable anywhere:** web browser first, then desktop, iPad/touch, and physical arcade installations.
- **Entertaining first:** fun, mood, and creative storytelling come before information.
- **Scientifically honest:** inspired by real research, with simplifications stated openly.
- **Accessible:** readable, controllable by keyboard or touch, colour-blind friendly, motion-sensitive aware.
- **Connected to people:** each game showcases real scientists through complementary materials (epilogue, web page, exhibit text).

## What we avoid
- Quiz-and-reward "edutainment" structures.
- Invented facts, exaggerated claims, or implied endorsements.
- Grinding, unlocks, or progression padding.
- Dark patterns, ads, or data collection inside games.

## Signature: "The Science Is Real"
Every game ends with a short, skippable discovery sequence that reveals the real organism or phenomenon, the research
behind it, and links to learn more. The reusable format is documented per game (`docs/ENDING_SEQUENCE.md`) and will be
standardized here once the first game ships it.

## Audiences
Casual players, science enthusiasts, classrooms, museums, and the scientific community itself.
