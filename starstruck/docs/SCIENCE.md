# STARSTRUCK. — Science notes

The game is inspired by published research on the star-nosed mole (_Condylura cristata_). Credit to the
researcher named in the brief is a **placeholder until he agrees** (public repository rule); until then the game
says only that it is inspired by published research, and implies no collaboration or endorsement.

## Status

- The ending now carries real scientific content (not placeholders) in `src/content/science.ts` (the "DID YOU
  KNOW?" reveal in `EndingScene.ts`): the touch fovea, reaction speed, and underwater sniffing, each with checked
  references. "Meet the Scientist" and "Credits" remain **placeholders** pending the contributing scientist's
  written approval, per this repo's public-credit rule.
- Energy, timers, scoring, the HUD, and the other playable characters (Noseferatu, Sherlock Moles, Molezilla,
  Sir Sniffs-a-Lot, The Starfather) are gameplay fiction, not measurements of any real animal.

## References (each checked against PubMed/the publisher before use)

- Catania, Kaas (1997). "Somatosensory Fovea in the Star-Nosed Mole: Behavioral Use of the Star in Relation to
  Innervation Patterns and Cortical Representation." _Journal of Comparative Neurology_ 387(2):215–233.
  PMID 9336224.
- Catania, Remple (2004). "Tactile Foveation in the Star-Nosed Mole." _Brain, Behavior and Evolution_
  63(1):1–12. PMID 14673194.
- Catania, Remple (2005). "Asymptotic Prey Profitability Drives Star-Nosed Moles to the Foraging Speed Limit."
  _Nature_ 433(7025):519–522. PMID 15690041.
- Catania (2006). "Olfaction: Underwater 'Sniffing' by Semi-Aquatic Mammals." _Nature_ 444(7122):1024–1025.
  PMID 17183311.

These four cover the facts the original brief relied on: the 22-rayed star and its touch fovea (ray 11), reaction
times fast enough to count as a speed limit for tactile processing, and real underwater sniffing — the direct
basis for the game's BUBBLE SNIFF mechanic. The brief's cortical-magnification and Eimer's-organ-count figures
are not yet individually cited and stay out of the reveal until they are.

## What the reveal does not claim

- That the mole in this game sees a HUD, hears music, or makes decisions the way the player does — its real
  counterpart has no vision to speak of and works through touch and smell alone.
- That any playable character other than the mole itself (Noseferatu, Sherlock Moles, Molezilla, Sir
  Sniffs-a-Lot, The Starfather) is drawn from a specific real species or study.
- That energy, timers, scoring, or combo mechanics correspond to any measured physiological limit.

## Gameplay abstractions (unchanged from the original brief)

- Glowing marks are a screen translation of touch; the mole does not see outlines.
- Ray angles, lengths, resolutions and tap rates are play values, not anatomy or physiology.
- Sensory memory, identification "evidence", energy and combos are game mechanics.
- Player reaction time is not the mole's handling time.
