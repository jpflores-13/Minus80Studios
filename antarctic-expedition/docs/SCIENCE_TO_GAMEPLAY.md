# Science to gameplay

Unlike some of this studio's other games, Antarctic Expedition's science is not a hidden mapping — it is
shown directly in the Discovery Journal and Scientific Mysteries. This table is the explicit link between
each verified finding and the mechanic that teaches it.

| Real finding                                                                                              | Where it shows up in-game                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Leopard seal courtship and calls, copulation never directly observed (Kienle et al., 2024)                | DIVE's leopard seal entry documents a "vocalizing underwater" behavior window; Scientific Mystery 1 asks whether this confirms mating, and the only correct call is "possible, but insufficient evidence"                                                                   |
| A novel, largely uncharacterized Antarctic viral seascape (Piedade et al., 2024)                          | ONE DROP's Viral Frontier scale and its Journal entry; Scientific Mystery 2                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Epifluorescence enumeration with SYBR Green I (Patel et al., 2007); counting vs. resolving structure      | ONE DROP's Microbial World scale; Scientific Mystery 6 ("counting vs. structure") contrasts counting with what RECONSTRUCT's cryo-EM scale does                                                                                                                             |
| Icefish lack hemoglobin                                                                                   | DIVE's icefish entry; Scientific Mystery 3                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Cryo-EM particle picking and averaging many images to reconstruct a structure                             | RECONSTRUCT's mechanic directly, plus its explicitly-illustrative reveal                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| The four-category classification itself (target/non-target/artifact/unknown, honest uncertainty rewarded) | Not a single paper — reflects how real microscopy and particle-picking workflows separate "what I'm looking for," "something else real," "an imaging artifact," and "genuinely unclear," and the norm that documenting uncertainty honestly is good practice, not a failure |

## What is simplified, stated plainly where it shows up

- DIVE's photo-quality formula, and every ONE DROP/RECONSTRUCT particle's radius, speed, and point value,
  are gameplay-balancing numbers (see `docs/SCIENCE.md`).
- RECONSTRUCT compresses a real reconstruction pipeline (thousands of averaged images) into one
  tap-or-flag decision per particle, and says so on its own reveal screen.
- Weddell seal, Adelie penguin, and krill swarm facts in the Journal are general natural history, not
  tied to a specific study — their Journal entries say this explicitly rather than implying a citation
  that does not exist.
